Privacy policy

Botmap for iPhone collects no data.

Last updated: 12 November 2026 (commercial licensing)

The developer receives nothing from the app: no account, no analytics, no tracking, no advertising.

What the app does

It shows the questions your own AI agents, run by Botmap on your Mac, are waiting on, and sends your answers back. It connects only to your own Mac, at the address in the pairing code Botmap on your Mac shows, for example over your own Tailscale network. Nothing passes through a server of ours.

Dictation

When you tap the microphone, your iPhone writes down what you say with Apple’s speech recognition, on the device where it can (Apple’s privacy policy applies). The recording is not kept and goes nowhere else; only the text you send goes to your Mac.

Calls

When you call your agents from the iPhone, the app asks you first, once. During a call your voice and what you and GPT Live say go from your iPhone straight to OpenAI, using the OpenAI key set in Botmap on your Mac (OpenAI’s privacy policy applies). What the call does on your map goes through your own Mac. Nothing goes through a server of ours, and nothing is kept by the app.

Local call history

Updated iPhone builds save call transcripts, timestamps, node references and action results locally on your own Mac for troubleshooting. Unsent text records wait in protected storage on the phone until the Mac is reachable. No raw audio is saved. Open these local files in Botmap for Mac under Settings → Phone call history; you can delete them there.

Camera and photos

Botmap does not save a photo when you scan a pairing code. When you take or select a photo to attach to a note, Botmap sends a copy to your paired Mac and stores it there for the note.

On the phone

The app keeps your Mac’s address, and the pairing key in the Keychain, so it can reconnect. Pair again or delete the app to remove them.

Botmap for Mac

Botmap for Mac runs on your Mac and keeps your map there. It reads the chats your agents keep on your Mac only to show and import them. If you set up an OpenAI key for dictation on the Mac, what you dictate there is sent to OpenAI to be written down (OpenAI’s privacy policy applies).

Optional desktop activity

In supported desktop builds, choose Settings → Share desktop activity to help Elias Pfeffer count active installations. When you interact with the app, it sends a random installation identifier. If Include approximate country is on, the collector uses the IP address of the connection to estimate your country. Turn either setting off at any time. The app sends no chats, project details or location coordinates for these statistics.

Botmap Tab Sharing browser extension

When you approve a tab, the people named in the sharing dialog can see its title and a live view of the page. This includes information visible on a signed-in website. Their clicks, scrolling and text edits take effect in your existing browser session. The website processes those actions just as it processes your own.

The extension sends tab images to the Botmap app on your computer. Botmap makes them available to the connected collaborators you approved. The extension keeps images in memory while sharing and processes incoming input there. It uses the browser’s session storage for pending requests and status messages. It does not request access to browser cookies or include advertising or analytics.

To stop sharing, choose “Freigabe beenden” in the extension or close the tab. Disconnecting a collaborator in Botmap ends that person’s access. Edits they already made on the website remain there.

This website

This site is plain pages: no analytics or advertising. The homepage uses local scripts for the film and copying the install command. Your browser also requests public download and star statistics from GitHub’s API. These requests use no credentials or referrer. The site is hosted on GitHub Pages; GitHub (GitHub, Inc., USA) handles the requests and may keep your IP address in its server logs to run and protect the service (GitHub’s privacy statement).
